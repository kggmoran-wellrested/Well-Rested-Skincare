The Body Edit · Routine Guide

The body care routine I rebuilt in my late 40s.

Why body skin ages differently, the daily minimum, the weekly layer, the targeted treatments, and the hands and décolletage conversation nobody is having. Products at three budget tiers.

By Katherine Moran
Published October 2026
11 min read

I started paying attention to my face in my 20s. I did not start paying attention to my body skin until my mid 40s, when the dryness showed up and no amount of the lotion I had used for a decade seemed to fix it. Then came the crepey texture on my upper arms. Then the dullness across my chest. Then the brown spots on my hands I had been ignoring.

What I figured out, slowly, is that the face routine I had been building for twenty years does not transfer to the rest of me. Body skin is different. The products that work on it are different. The timing is different. And the areas most of us ignore (hands, neck, chest) are the ones that show midlife changes the fastest.

This is the routine I rebuilt. Why body skin ages differently, the three-step daily minimum, the weekly layer, the targeted treatments, and the honest conversation about hands and décolletage. Products at three tiers so you can start wherever your budget allows.

Why body skin ages differently than your face.

Three things make body skin a different conversation from face skin.

Fewer oil glands. Your face has the highest concentration of sebaceous glands in your body. Your arms, legs, and back have far fewer. That means body skin is drier at baseline and more dependent on topical moisture to stay comfortable. The drop in natural oil production that happens in perimenopause hits body skin harder because there was less to begin with.

Thinner skin in the places that show. The skin on your chest, neck, and the backs of your hands is some of the thinnest skin on your body. Combined with cumulative sun exposure (because almost no one applies SPF to their chest the way they apply it to their face), these are the areas where visible aging shows up first and most dramatically.

Decades of unprotected UV exposure. I started wearing face SPF daily in my early 30s. I did not start wearing body SPF until my mid 40s. That gap of fifteen years is etched into the skin on my hands and chest in a way that would not have happened if I had treated the two surfaces the same. Most women in my generation share that history.

Perimenopause accelerates all three. Estrogen decline reduces collagen production, slows cell turnover, and compromises barrier lipid synthesis across the whole body. The result is the dryness, the loss of firmness, the texture changes, and the pigmentation that often show up in your 40s all at once.

The three shifts most of us notice
  • Dryness that regular lotion is not fixing. The barrier needs more than it did ten years ago.
  • Crepey texture on arms, chest, and the backs of hands. Collagen loss showing on the surface.
  • Pigmentation and dullness. Years of unprotected UV finally becoming visible.

The daily minimum: three steps.

If you do nothing else from this article, do these three things every day.

Step 1 · Gentle body wash

The body wash you grew up with was probably a sulfate-based foaming cleanser designed to strip oil. That is the opposite of what midlife skin needs. Swap to a non-stripping, barrier-safe formula with glycerin, ceramides, or hyaluronic acid. You should finish a shower feeling clean, not tight.

What to look for: cream, oil, or syndet (synthetic detergent) base. Avoid sulfates and heavy fragrance. Dove Advanced Care Deep Moisture Body Wash is the affordable pick that holds up to clinical scrutiny. Nécessaire The Body Wash is the mid-tier upgrade with proper ceramides.

Step 2 · Daily body moisturizer

Apply within three minutes of toweling off, while skin is still damp. This locks in the water that will otherwise evaporate within the first ten minutes after your shower. If you remember nothing else about moisturizing, remember this: the first ten minutes count more than the next ten hours.

What to look for: ceramides, glycerin, hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, or urea in the first five ingredients. Avoid heavy fragrance if your skin is reactive. For most people, a pump lotion is more realistic than a jar cream (ease of application matters as much as formula).

CeraVe Moisturizing Lotion for Dry Skin is the dermatologist-recommended affordable. Nécessaire The Body Lotion is the mid-tier upgrade. Augustinus Bader The Body Cream is the splurge for anyone who wants their body skin to feel like their face routine.

Step 3 · Body SPF

Non-optional on any skin that will see sun that day. Hands and chest especially. The UV damage you accumulate on these areas across decades is why they age faster than any other part of your body except the face.

What to look for: SPF 30 or higher, broad-spectrum, in a lightweight lotion format designed for body (not a thick face SPF). Dedicated body SPF absorbs faster and does not leave the greasy feel of face sunscreen stretched across your arms.

EltaMD UV Lotion Broad-Spectrum SPF 30+ is the dermatologist pick. Supergoop Play Everyday Lotion SPF 50 is a solid mid-tier option. For hands and chest specifically, apply SPF daily even in winter.

The weekly layer: chemical exfoliation.

Once your daily routine is locked in, the next lever is chemical exfoliation. For body skin, this is where the biggest texture and tone improvements come from.

Body chemical exfoliants work the same way face acids do. They dissolve the bonds holding dead cells on the surface, which brightens tone, smooths texture, fades mild pigmentation, and helps skincare that goes on afterward penetrate better. The difference is that body skin tolerates stronger concentrations and larger surface applications than face skin.

Three acids do most of the work on body:

Lactic acid hydrates while it exfoliates. The gentlest of the three, best for first-time users and sensitive skin. Amlactin Daily Moisturizing Lotion at 12% is the classic that has been in dermatology recommendations for thirty years. Use daily on arms, legs, and anywhere else you want smoother texture.

Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, which lets it penetrate into clogged pores and bumpy spots. Best for keratosis pilaris, back acne, and chest breakouts. CeraVe SA Lotion for Rough and Bumpy Skin is the gentle option. Paula's Choice 2% BHA Body Spray reaches areas you cannot otherwise cover.

Urea is a dual-action ingredient that hydrates at low concentrations and exfoliates at higher ones. Prequel Urea 10% Moisturizing Lotion works for both face and body, which makes it a strong single-product addition for anyone with dryness and texture at the same time.

How to add exfoliation without blowing out your barrier

Start with one acid, two to three times a week. Build to daily if your skin tolerates it. If you see redness or irritation, drop back to twice a week and pair with a ceramide moisturizer on off nights. The chemical peel menu I put together covers the full progression from gentlest to strongest if you want more depth on how acids work.

If you have keratosis pilaris

The bumpy upper arms, back-of-thigh roughness, and small flesh-colored or red bumps that may surface in your 40s even if you did not have them earlier. The two levers that smooth KP are daily lactic acid or urea lotions, and a weekly chemical exfoliating scrub like First Aid Beauty KP Bump Eraser. Give it four to six weeks before judging whether it is working. KP is slow to shift and comes back if you stop.

Targeted treatments: vitamin C, retinol, and firming for body.

If daily routine and weekly exfoliation are in place and you want more, this is the next layer.

Body vitamin C for pigmentation and brightening

The same vitamin C that works on your face works on your body, just in body-friendly formulations. Nécessaire The Body Serum combines vitamin C with niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, and peptides in one bottle. The spots on my chest from fifteen years of unprotected sun lightened in six weeks of twice-daily application. Nécessaire The Body Vitamin C is the stronger splurge option for anyone who wants a more aggressive brightening treatment.

Body retinol and peptide treatments for crepey skin

Crepey skin on arms and chest responds to the same actives that work on face: retinoids and peptides. Body-specific formulations are generally lower concentrations than face retinoids because body skin covers a bigger surface area and higher strengths would be irritating.

Alastin TransFORM Body Treatment with TriHex Technology is the splurge with the strongest clinical data for post-weight-loss and post-GLP-1 body skin. It uses the same technology family as the Alastin Restorative Skin Complex my dermatologist is firm about around CO2 laser treatments. Results build over three to six months.

For a more accessible option, any face peptide serum you already use can be applied to your chest and the backs of your hands. These are the two areas where facial-grade actives make the biggest visible difference per application.

Hands, neck, and décolletage: the honest conversation.

If you do everything else from this article but ignore your hands, neck, and chest, you will still look older than your face suggests. These three areas carry the most visible signs of aging in midlife and get the least attention.

The hands

Hands have thin skin, high UV exposure, constant washing, and almost no fat padding by the time you reach your 40s. The pigmentation, texture, and crepiness that show up here are the result of all four. The daily minimum for hands is a dedicated hand cream applied after every hand wash. Keep it on your kitchen counter, nightstand, desk, and in your bag. CeraVe Therapeutic Hand Cream is the daily workhorse. L'Occitane Shea Butter Hand Cream is the splurge that lasts through more hand washings before you need to reapply.

For pigmentation on hands, treat them the same way you treat face pigmentation. Daily SPF 50 (which hand creams rarely include), nightly retinoid applied with the backs of hands facing up, and vitamin C in the morning. The pigmentation stack I use works on hands too.

The neck and décolletage

The neck and chest are thinner than face skin and often more sun-damaged. Apply everything you apply to your face down to your clavicle and across your chest. Vitamin C in the morning, retinoid at night, SPF daily. The products do not need to be different, just the discipline to extend them past your jawline.

One addition worth considering for the chest specifically is a facial-grade peptide or growth factor serum applied in the evening. The chest has fewer oil glands than the face and often responds dramatically to treatment because it has rarely received any.

If you are on a GLP-1: the body conversation.

If you are reading this and you are on Ozempic, Mounjaro, Wegovy, or another GLP-1, the body changes are a separate chapter.

GLP-1 medications cause fat loss throughout the body, including the subcutaneous fat compartments that give arms, abdomen, and legs their structure. When that fat compartments shrinks, the skin above it loses the support it was resting on. The result is loose or crepey skin that was not there before you started the medication.

The two levers that help most are time (collagen remodeling continues for 12 to 24 months after weight stabilization) and consistent treatment. For body skin on GLP-1s specifically, Alastin TransFORM has the strongest clinical data. For face changes on GLP-1s, the Ozempic Face Protocol covers the full plan.

Daily moisturizer with peptides, body SPF, chemical exfoliation two to three times a week, and a firming body treatment like Alastin or Nécessaire The Body Serum are the core components. Expect to see change over months, not weeks.

Putting it together: a sample weekly routine.

Here is what a realistic week looks like.

Every morning: gentle body wash in the shower, moisturizer within three minutes of toweling off, body SPF on anything that will see sun. Hand cream after your first hand wash. If you have time, apply a peptide or vitamin C serum to your chest.

Every evening: moisturizer again if your skin feels dry. Retinoid or peptide serum to the chest and backs of hands. Hand cream before bed.

Two to three times a week: swap your regular daily moisturizer for a lactic acid or salicylic acid lotion on the arms, legs, and anywhere with texture or KP. Follow with ceramide moisturizer if your skin feels dry after.

Once a week: chemical exfoliating body scrub or weekly peel treatment on arms, legs, and back if accessible. Follow with a rich moisturizer. Give skin a recovery night afterward.

The routine is less interesting than the discipline. Three to six months of consistent body care produces more visible change than six months of expensive single-product experiments.

Bottom Line

Body care is where the biggest change-per-dollar hides.

Face routines are oversaturated. Body routines are underbuilt. The three-step daily minimum (gentle wash, barrier moisturizer, body SPF) is the foundation. Weekly chemical exfoliation is the lever that moves texture. Targeted treatments on hands, neck, and chest are what keeps your body skin visually aligned with your face. GLP-1 users need the additional firming layer. Everything responds to three to six months of consistent use. Nothing responds to a week.
