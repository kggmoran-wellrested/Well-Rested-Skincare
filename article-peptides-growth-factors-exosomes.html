Ingredient Truth · Expensive Actives

The truth about peptides, growth factors, and exosomes for midlife skin.

What they do, where they fit in your routine, and whether they earn the price. Includes a Plated vs INVO Aesthetics comparison for anyone shopping the exosome category.

By Katherine Moran
Published September 2026
12 min read

I have used peptides, growth factors, and exosomes for years. I like the products. I could not tell you if any of them made a clear, dramatic change to my skin. And for a long time I could not tell you where each one was supposed to sit in a routine.

That is the problem with this category. The prices are high, the science is slow, and nobody explains what to expect or where to put the bottle when you get it home. So you end up with a $150 to $250 serum on your shelf, using it inconsistently, hoping it is doing something.

This is what I have learned after using five of them across the three categories. In plain language, with realistic timelines, and with a framework for deciding whether to spend the money.

Peptides, growth factors, and exosomes are three different things.

The category gets treated like one thing on shelves and in ads. It is not.

Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as signals. They tell your skin cells to do something specific: produce more collagen, calm inflammation, tighten temporarily, or support the barrier. Peptides are the simplest of the three categories and the most affordable at the entry level.

There are dozens of named peptides, and most modern serums use a blend of five to ten of them. Palmitoyl tripeptide-1 signals collagen production. Copper peptides support wound repair. Argireline mimics botulinum toxin at a very mild level and softens the appearance of dynamic lines. Each one is a small message the skin is asked to hear.

Growth factors are larger proteins that support cell repair, division, and regeneration. Where peptides are signals, growth factors are the workers. Your body produces them constantly and the levels decline as estrogen declines. Topical growth factors, in theory, top up what the body is no longer making at the same rate.

Growth factor products are often derived from plant sources, bacterial fermentation, or, in medical-grade lines like Neocutis, from cultured human skin cells. This last category is the most studied and the most expensive, and it also requires cold chain shipping and short shelf lives to stay stable.

Exosomes are the newest of the three. They are tiny vesicles, roughly one hundred times smaller than a human cell, that carry cargo between cells: growth factors, RNA, signaling molecules, lipids. Think of them as tiny FedEx trucks delivering messages and materials. Your body uses them for repair, immune response, and communication.

In skincare, exosomes are usually derived from platelet-rich plasma, stem cells, or plant sources. Plated is one of the leading brands in this space and uses platelet-derived exosomes. The idea is that applying exosomes topically delivers the full package of repair signals in one shot, rather than delivering one growth factor at a time.

That is why exosomes are the most expensive of the three. Manufacturing them is complex and the category is early.

The three categories at a glance
  • Peptides: signal molecules that tell skin cells to do specific jobs. Affordable, layerable, foundational.
  • Growth factors: larger proteins that support cell repair and regeneration. Priced high, work slowly, need consistency.
  • Exosomes: tiny delivery vehicles that carry growth factors and signaling molecules together. Newest, most expensive, best around procedures.

Why "does it work" is the wrong question.

Here is what nobody says out loud about these three categories. They do not work the way a retinoid works.

A retinoid produces a visible change over eight to twelve weeks. Your skin gets a little pink, then it settles, then your pores look smaller and your skin looks smoother and any pigmentation lightens. You can point to the change. You can see it in a photo.

Peptides, growth factors, and exosomes are slower and less obvious. They support processes rather than driving them. They preserve rather than transform. And the timeline for noticing anything is closer to six to twelve months than eight to twelve weeks.

The other truth is that these products are best measured by absence. Absence of decline. Absence of the fine lines that would have shown up if you had not been supporting collagen. Absence of the redness spike after a procedure. Absence of the visible aging that would have happened without daily support.

That is a much harder result to see in the mirror than a smoother pore or a lightened spot. It also means the honest answer to "does it work" is "probably, but you will only know in retrospect."

The exception is around procedures. If you microneedle at home or in office, or if you have a laser or biostimulator treatment, growth factors and exosomes are one of the few categories where you can see a measurable difference in how your skin heals. That is where the science is clearest and the visible result is most direct.

Where each one fits in a routine.

Now for the practical part. Here is where each category goes in a real midlife routine.

Peptides go in every night, and they layer with everything.

Peptides are the friendliest active in this category. They do not compete with retinoids, they do not irritate, and they do not require special pairing rules. Apply your peptide serum on clean dry skin after your other actives have absorbed, before your moisturizer. If you are new to actives and want one non-negotiable, this is it.

Growth factors go in the evening, on retinoid-off nights, or layered after the retinoid has absorbed.

Growth factors work best when they are not competing with harsh actives. On the nights I use a retinoid, I apply the growth factor as a layer over the top after the retinoid has soaked in for ten minutes. On non-retinoid nights, I use the growth factor first, then a peptide serum or a barrier moisturizer. Do not use them the same night as strong AHAs or BHAs. That combination irritates rather than helps.

Exosomes have two useful moments: pre-procedure prep and post-procedure recovery.

If you are heading into a microneedling session, a laser treatment, or a biostimulator like Sculptra, using exosomes in the two weeks before and the two weeks after is where the category earns most of its price. That is when the delivery mechanism is doing the most useful work. Between procedures, exosomes can go into your evening routine as a targeted serum, but the daily benefit is less pronounced than what you get around a procedure.

The at-home exosome category is growing: Plated vs INVO.

My sister recently picked up a bottle of INVO Aesthetics Rejuvenating Complex at her dermatologist's office. Her derm positioned it as better than Plated. She asked me what the difference was, and since I have been using Plated for a while, I went and did the research so I could give her an honest answer. If her derm is stocking INVO, there is a good chance yours is too, so here is what I found.

Plated and INVO Aesthetics are both at-home daily-use exosome products, but they are built from different source material and designed for different jobs.

The source and formulation are different.

Plated uses platelet-derived exosomes, made from platelet-rich plasma. It is a focused delivery product: exosomes and support ingredients, engineered to work through intact skin on a daily basis. The clinical data behind it is the strongest at-home exosome data currently available.

INVO Aesthetics uses a broader blend called BioBlend Technology, which combines exosomes with growth factors, peptides, niacinamide, carnosine, and honeybee defensin in one bottle. The exosomes are sourced from human fibroblasts, umbilical cord tissue (Wharton's jelly), and bone marrow mesenchymal cells. That is a wider biological source and a wider active ingredient list.

The price is different.

Plated Intense Serum is about $200 for a 30ml bottle. INVO Rejuvenating Complex is $285 for the same size. The premium buys you a broader formulation, not necessarily a stronger result.

Which is "better."

Neither has been shown in a head-to-head study to outperform the other on skin metrics. Plated has more independent clinical data behind it. INVO has more ingredients and higher marketed concentration numbers. If you are the kind of person who prefers a focused product with a clear mechanism, Plated is the cleaner choice. If you like the idea of layered actives in one bottle and are willing to pay for the convenience, INVO makes sense.

The one thing I would not do is stack them. Both products are working the same category on the same skin, and using them together is redundant. Pick one, use it consistently for six months, and take photos to see what you notice.

How to use INVO if you have it

Twice a day, on clean skin, before moisturizer. Compatible with retinoids if you space them by 15 to 20 minutes. Give it six to twelve weeks before deciding whether to reorder, and take photos at the start so you have something to compare to.

There is also a growing category of in-office exosome products used post-procedure for healing rather than as daily take-home serums. Those are a separate conversation and I will cover that category in a follow-up piece.

Where to spend, and where to hold back.

Here is my framework, based on what I have used.

Peptides at the affordable tier. The Ordinary Multi-Peptide + HA Serum, $15. Consistency is more important than concentration in this category, and $15 removes every excuse not to use it every night.

Growth factors at the investment or splurge tier if you are ready to commit. If you are going to use a growth factor product, use it every day for six months. Anything less than that and you will be in my situation, wondering if it works because you never gave it a real chance. Neocutis Bio Serum Firm is a legitimate splurge. If you are not sure you will use it daily, do not spend the money yet.

Exosomes for daily at-home use. Plated Intense Serum is my pick at around $200. INVO Aesthetics Rejuvenating Complex is a broader-formulation alternative at $285 through dermatologists. Pick one, not both. If you are microneedling or getting laser treatments, either one earns a bottle around each session. If you are not doing procedures at all and you are early in your active routine, exosomes are not the first place I would put your money. Get retinoid, vitamin C, peptides, and barrier repair locked in first.

Where the money should go first. Before any of these three categories, get a retinoid you tolerate, a vitamin C serum you use every morning, a barrier-supportive moisturizer, and daily SPF 50. Those four things are foundational. Peptides, growth factors, and exosomes support those four. They do not replace them.

If your foundation is not in place yet, spend there. Come back to this article once the basics are locked in.

The tier framework at a glance

Peptides: The Ordinary Multi-Peptide + HA Serum, $15, nightly. Upgrade only when you have a reason.

Growth factors: Neocutis Bio Serum Firm at splurge, daily use for six months minimum.

Exosomes: Plated Intense Serum ($200) or INVO Aesthetics ($285). Pick one. Best around procedures.

Realistic expectations.

Here is what I wish someone had told me at 42, standing in the SkinCeuticals aisle deciding whether the peptide bottle was worth it.

Peptides work over months. Growth factors work over months. Exosomes work over months, and most visibly around procedures. None of these three categories give you a before-and-after photo in eight weeks. If that is what you are hoping for, you will be disappointed, and you will assume the products failed when the timeline was the problem.

Give any of them six months of consistent use before you decide. Take photos at the start, at three months, at six months. Compare then. Not day to day.

And if you cannot tell whether a product is doing anything after six months of daily use, take that as data. It might not be your product. It might be that the category is less obvious than the marketing suggests, and the value is in the accumulated support over years rather than the visible result in a season.

That has been my experience. It might be yours too. And knowing that in advance is worth more than any single serum.

Bottom Line

Spend where the science is clearest and the daily use is realistic.

Peptides at the affordable tier are legitimate and worth using every night. Growth factors earn their price only if you use them daily for six months. Exosomes earn their price around procedures more than as a standalone daily serum. Between Plated and INVO Aesthetics, either one works, but pick one and give it six months. And before any of these three categories, lock in the foundation: retinoid, vitamin C, moisturizer, SPF. That is where the visible change comes from. Peptides, growth factors, and exosomes support that foundation. They do not replace it.
