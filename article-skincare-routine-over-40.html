The skincare routine for women over 40: what to use, in what order

Five essentials to start with, what to add for your main concern, and how to rotate the stronger steps through the week so your skin gets results without getting raw.

By Katherine
October 2026
9 min read

Somewhere in my 40s, the routine that had worked for twenty years stopped working. My skin got drier, products I had used forever started to sting, and the dark spots I used to ignore stopped fading on their own. My first instinct was to buy something new. What helped was the opposite: fewer products, in the right order, with the strong ones taking turns. This is the routine I would hand a friend who just noticed the same changes, from the five essentials to start with, to what to add later, to how the week fits together.

01
Why it changes

Why your routine stopped working

It is not your imagination, and it is not that your products got worse. As estrogen falls through perimenopause, your skin makes fewer of the lipids that hold water in, so it dries out faster and gets more reactive. Cell turnover slows down, which leaves skin looking duller and lets pigment linger. Collagen drops too, and that shows up as softer edges along the jaw and lines that stay after you stop smiling.

Those changes are why a routine built for your 30s often stops fitting. A strong acid you used every night can suddenly leave you raw, and a light gel moisturizer may not be enough anymore. The fix is usually not a whole new shelf. It is a gentler base, one strong active at a time, and a schedule that gives your skin room to recover. I go deeper into the hormone side in why your skincare stopped working in perimenopause.

What the research shows

Studies of postmenopausal skin have found that it can lose around 30% of its collagen in the first five years after menopause, with the steepest drop early on. That is a big reason the years around 45 to 55 feel so different from the decade before.

02
Start here

Start with five essentials

If you only do five things, these are the five I would choose. Together they clean without stripping, protect during the day, support your barrier, and do the slow repair work at night.

A gentle cleanser, morning and night. Choose one that leaves your face comfortable, not squeaky. If it feels tight afterward, it is too strong for midlife skin. On sunscreen or makeup days, an oil or balm first at night makes the second cleanse easier.

Vitamin C in the morning. It is an antioxidant that helps your sunscreen do its job, supports collagen, and over time softens the look of dark spots. If the classic L-ascorbic acid form stings, a gentler form is easier to stick with.

A moisturizer you like. Ceramides, glycerin and peptides are all good signs on the label. You can use the same one morning and night, and use a little more at night. A separate night cream is a nice extra, not a requirement.

Sunscreen every morning, last. Broad spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, about a quarter teaspoon for your face. If I could keep only one step, it would be this one, because sun undoes the work of everything else.

A retinoid at night. Retinoids have more research behind them for fine lines, texture and uneven tone than anything else you can buy without a prescription. Start with two nights a week and a pea-sized amount, then build up over a month or two. My retinol guide covers how to start without peeling.

What the research shows

In a 2013 trial in the Annals of Internal Medicine, adults who used sunscreen every day showed 24% less skin aging over four and a half years than adults who used it whenever they felt like it. Daily use, not perfect use, made the difference.

03
Weeks four to six

Add the rest once your skin settles

Once the retinoid feels easy, usually after four to six weeks, you can add three more steps. I would add them one at a time and give each a couple of weeks, so if something irritates you, you know which product it was.

An exfoliant, once a week to start. A gentle acid helps with texture and dullness, and it lets your other products work a little better. Lactic and mandelic acid are easier on dry or sensitive skin. Salicylic acid suits oily skin and enlarged pores. Never use it on the same night as your retinoid.

A peptide serum on your off nights. Peptides support your barrier and firmness without irritation, which makes them a good fit for the nights you skip the retinoid. My guide to peptides, growth factors and exosomes explains the differences.

One booster serum for your main concern. This is the step that targets what bothers you most. One is plenty. The next section covers which one to choose.

04
Your main concern

Choose one booster for what bothers you most

Your whole routine helps with all of these. The booster is the extra push, and it usually goes on in the morning after vitamin C.

Dark spots and uneven tone

Tranexamic acid and arbutin, ideally together. Sunscreen still does most of the work here, so do not skip it. Give it two to three months. My pigmentation stack goes further.

Redness

Azelaic acid calms redness and is gentle enough for sensitive and breakout-prone skin. Keep the rest of your routine calm too. More in my azelaic acid guide.

Firmness

Growth factors, often listed as EGF. Your retinoid and peptide serum are the workhorses for firmness, and this adds support. Think in months, not weeks.

Fine lines

Peptides such as argireline target the look of expression lines. Your retinoid is still the step with the most research behind it.

Two concerns, one product

If you have both dark spots and redness, azelaic acid helps with both, so one booster covers you. If you have both firmness and fine lines, a growth factor serum covers both. For other pairs, start with the booster for spots or redness, since your retinoid and peptide serum are already working on lines and firmness.

05
The schedule

Your week: let the strong steps take turns

Mornings stay the same every day. At night, the retinoid and the exfoliant never share a night, and the other nights give your skin a break. Cleanse first and finish with moisturizer every night.

SunRecovery

If you are new to retinoids, make Saturday a peptide night for the first month, then add it back as a third retinoid night once your skin is calm. Sunday is a recovery night: peptide serum and your richest moisturizer, nothing strong. If you use an LED mask, it goes on clean, dry skin before your serums on the nights you choose.

If your skin turns red or flaky, or starts to sting, swap in recovery nights for a few days and pick back up when it settles. Irritation usually means too much too soon, not that a product is wrong for you.

06
Your skin type

Adjust it for your skin

Dry skin: a cream cleanser, a richer moisturizer, and a gentle lactic or mandelic acid. A separate rich night cream is worth it here, especially after 55.

Sensitive skin: a gentler vitamin C, a mineral sunscreen, and patience with the retinoid. Patch test anything new on your jaw for a few nights first.

Oily or combination skin: a gel cleanser, a lighter moisturizer, and salicylic acid as your exfoliant. Midlife skin can be oily and dehydrated at the same time, so do not skip moisturizer.

07
Budget

Where to spend, and where to save

A good routine does not have to be expensive. If you want to splurge somewhere, put the money into the steps that stay on your skin and do the active work: vitamin C, your retinoid and your booster. Save on the cleanser, which rinses off in a minute, and on moisturizer and sunscreen, where plenty of affordable formulas are excellent.

As a rough guide, the five essentials usually run about $100 to $150 with budget picks, and the full routine about $175 to $275. Splurging only where it counts lands in between budget and full luxury. Prices change, so check current prices before you buy.

The bottom line

Skincare after 40 works best as a plan, not a pile of products. Start with a gentle cleanser, vitamin C, moisturizer, sunscreen and a retinoid. Give it four to six weeks. Then add an exfoliant, a peptide serum and one booster for what bothers you most, and let the strong steps take turns through the week.

Common questions

What is the best skincare routine for women over 40?

Start with five essentials: a gentle cleanser morning and night, vitamin C in the morning, a moisturizer, sunscreen every morning, and a retinoid two to three nights a week. After four to six weeks, add an exfoliant once a week, a peptide serum on your off nights, and one booster serum for your main concern.

What order should I apply skincare in after 40?

In the morning: cleanser, vitamin C, booster serum, moisturizer, then sunscreen last. At night: cleanser, then your retinoid, exfoliant or peptide serum depending on the night, then moisturizer last. In general, thinner products go on before thicker ones.

Can I use vitamin C and retinol in the same routine?

Yes. Use vitamin C in the morning, when its antioxidant help pairs well with sunscreen, and your retinoid at night. Keeping them apart also lowers the chance of irritation.

How long until I see results?

Most people notice brighter, more comfortable skin within a few weeks. Changes in fine lines, dark spots and firmness take longer, usually around two to three months of steady use, and retinoid results keep building after that.

This is general education from my own research and routine, not medical advice for your skin. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, skip the retinoid and ask your doctor what to use instead, and check with your dermatologist if you have a skin condition or use prescription skincare.
