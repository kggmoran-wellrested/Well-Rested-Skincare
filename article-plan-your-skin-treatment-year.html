How to plan a year of skin treatments, without wasting a season

Picking the treatment is half of it. The other half is when you book it, what you pause around it and how much of your calendar it takes.

By Katherine
November 2026
8 min read

I have had two rounds of fractional CO2 and I do Sculptra twice a year. The treatments themselves were the easy part to choose. What took me longer to learn was the planning: which month to book, how many days to clear, what to stop using beforehand, and how to keep one treatment from getting in the way of the next.

Start with the season, not the menu

Many dermatology practices suggest fall and winter for laser resurfacing and deeper peels. The reason is the sun. Freshly treated skin is more sensitive to UV, and a tan going into treatment raises the chance of dark marks afterward. Shorter days and more time indoors make both of those easier to manage.

That gives the year a natural shape. I plan my CO2 for fall, and I treat spring and summer as the maintenance months.

  • FallThe window for the strongest treatments, once the summer tan has faded. Book consults in late summer, because good providers fill up.
  • WinterRecovery, follow-up visits and any gentle series that needs several sessions a few weeks apart.
  • SpringFinish the last sessions early, then shift to daily sunscreen, your home routine and at-home devices.
  • SummerProtect what you did. This is the season to research providers and decide what next fall looks like.

Count backward from your calendar

Before I book anything, I write down the dates I cannot look like I am healing: weddings, work trips, school events, photos. Then I count backward. Downtime depends on how strong the treatment is.

Two things are easy to miss. First, downtime is when you can go out, not when you look your best. Pinkness can linger after the peeling stops, so leave a cushion before anything important. Second, the result keeps building after you heal. With my first round of CO2, the biggest improvement showed up at about three months. If you want to look your best for something in June, a treatment in May is too late.

A series takes longer than you think

Gentler lasers usually come as a series of several sessions spaced weeks apart. Four sessions a month apart is a whole season. If you start in March, you finish in summer sun. Start a series in fall or early winter so the last session lands before spring.

What to pause, and when

Every provider gives slightly different instructions, and theirs are the ones to follow. These are the patterns I see again and again, so you can plan for them.

  • Sun and tanningPlan on avoiding sun for at least two weeks before treatment. Some instructions ask for four weeks or longer, and for careful sun protection for weeks afterward. A recent tan or self-tanner can mean rescheduling.
  • Retinoids and exfoliating acidsThese are commonly paused several days to a week before, and you restart only when your provider says so.
  • At-home devicesI stop mine around a treatment and ask at the consult when I can start again. Write the restart date down so it does not drift.
  • Other treatmentsI do not book my CO2 and my Sculptra close together. Ask your provider how far apart yours should be, and tell every provider what the others are doing.
  • Cold sores and medicationsIf you get cold sores, ask whether you need medicine ahead of time. Bring a list of everything you take.

While you heal, the routine gets very short: a gentle cleanser, a plain barrier cream and mineral sunscreen once you are cleared for it. My post-procedure guide has the products I used across both rounds of CO2.

Budget for the whole plan

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists an average fee of $1,829 for skin resurfacing procedures such as laser, and notes that the average does not include related costs. Prices vary widely by city, device and provider, so use any number you read online, including that one, as a starting point.

What helped me was pricing the year, not the visit. A series multiplies the per-session price. Aftercare products, numbing, follow-up visits and time off work are all part of the cost. Ask for the total for the full plan in writing, and ask whether a package is cheaper than paying per session. My comparison chart has typical per-session prices by treatment.

The consult and booking checklist

I keep this list in my planner and work through it in order. For the questions to ask the provider about the device and their experience, use the list in my laser article.

Before you call

  • Name one goalBrown spots, texture, laxity or redness. One clear goal makes the consult far more useful.
  • Mark your blackout datesEvents, travel and anything outdoors for the next six months.
  • List your routineEvery active you use, every device, and any other treatments you have had in the last year.

At the consult

  • How many sessions, how far apart?Then check that the last one lands before spring.
  • What do I stop, and on what date?Ask for exact dates for retinoids, acids, devices and sun.
  • What will I look like on day 3, day 7 and day 14?This tells you how much time to block.
  • When can I restart each product?Get it in writing.
  • What is the total cost?Sessions, numbing, aftercare and follow-ups.

The week before

  • Stop what you were told to stopOn the dates you were given.
  • Stock your recovery shelfGentle cleanser, barrier cream, mineral sunscreen, clean pillowcases.
  • Take a before photoSame window, same time of day, no makeup.
  • Clear your calendarIncluding workouts and anything outdoors.

After

  • Log itDate, provider, device, settings if they will share them, cost and how each day of recovery went.
  • Book the follow-upAnd set a reminder to take a comparison photo at three months.

A treatment plan is a calendar first. The device is the second decision.

The bottom line

Pick the season before you pick the treatment. Count backward from the dates you care about, and leave more room than you think you need. Get your pause and restart dates in writing, price the whole plan, and write everything down so next year is easier to plan than this one.
