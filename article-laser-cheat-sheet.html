The laser cheat sheet: every name, what it is and what it targets

The question I get most about lasers is about the names. Here they are on one page, with the type of laser, the skin goal and the typical cost.

By Katherine
November 2026
4 min read

Since I shared my CO2 laser recovery, the question I keep getting is some version of this: what is the difference between all of these lasers? Most of the confusion comes from the names. Some treatments go by a brand name, some go by the type of laser, and the same laser can show up on two menus under two different names.

Brand name or type of laser?

The type tells you what the device does. The brand name is what one company calls its version. CO2 is a type. CoolPeel, CO2RE and DeepFX are brand names for CO2 lasers used at different strengths. Moxi and Clear + Brilliant are different brands of the same gentle type. When a menu lists a name you do not know, ask what type of laser it is and you can find it on this chart.

IPL uses broad light and RF microneedling uses radiofrequency energy, but both are booked alongside lasers so often that they belong here. Heat can make melasma worse, so if you have it, talk with your provider before booking anything on this list.

Find it by skin goal

  • Redness and broken vesselsVbeam, or IPL and BBL. Laser Genesis is a gentler option for diffuse redness.
  • Brown spots and sun damageIPL or BBL and pico lasers for spots. Moxi, Fraxel Dual and Halo for sun damage across the whole face.
  • Glow, pores and upkeepClear + Brilliant, Laser Genesis or Moxi.
  • Fine lines and textureCoolPeel, erbium or Fraxel Dual, with Halo as the step up.
  • Deeper wrinkles, crepey skin and scarsFractional CO2 is the strongest option. It is the one I have had, twice.
  • LaxityLasers resurface more than they lift. RF microneedling is the one on this chart aimed at firmness.
  • MelasmaGo slowly, because heat can trigger it. My peel menu covers gentler options to discuss with your provider.
Before you book

A laser is only as good as the person running it. Ask who will do the treatment, how many of this exact treatment they have done, how much experience they have with your skin tone, and what the total cost includes. My guide to planning a year of skin treatments has a full consult checklist.
