Routine Guide

How to build a nighttime skincare routine, step by step

Evening is when retinoids and exfoliants can work without the sun undoing them. Here is the order I would follow, plus a simple weekly schedule so you never use too much at once.

By Katherine
Updated October 2026
8 min read

Your skin does a lot of its repair work overnight, and an evening routine is how you support it. With no sunscreen to protect and no makeup to wear, night is also the best time for the strongest actives, especially retinoids and exfoliating acids, since both make skin more sensitive to the sun. If you have read my piece on sleep and skin aging, this is the practical side of it.

The mistake I made for years was trying to use everything every night. A good evening routine spreads its strongest steps across the week so your skin gets results without getting raw. Below are the five steps in order, followed by a schedule you can copy.

The Nighttime Routine, Step by Step
01CleanseTwice if you wore SPF
02Exfoliant1 to 3 nights a week
03Treatment SerumPeptides or exosomes
04RetinoidOn non-exfoliant nights
05MoisturizerRicher at night
01
Step One

Cleanse: twice if you wore sunscreen

Everything else in your evening routine works better on clean skin. If you wore sunscreen or makeup, a double cleanse is the easiest way to get it all off: massage a cleansing oil or balm onto dry skin for about a minute and rinse, then follow with a gentle water-based cleanser.

On days you stayed home without sunscreen, one gentle cleanse is enough. Either way, skip hot water and rough washcloths, and pat your skin dry rather than rubbing it.

Why the oil step

Sunscreen and long-wear makeup are made to stay put, so a single water-based wash often leaves some behind. Oil breaks them down first, which lets the second cleanser finish the job without any scrubbing.

02
Step Two

Exfoliant: one to three nights a week

Chemical exfoliants loosen the dead cells that make skin look dull and rough, and they help even out tone over time. They belong at night because acids like glycolic and lactic acid make skin more sensitive to the sun.

Start with one night a week and build to two or three if your skin stays comfortable. Apply your exfoliant to dry skin after cleansing, and skip your retinoid that night, since using both together is the fastest way to irritated, peeling skin.

Which acid to choose

Lactic acid is the gentlest AHA and a good place to start, since it also helps hold in moisture. Glycolic acid works faster but stings more. Salicylic acid, a BHA, is best for clogged pores and breakouts. PHAs are the mildest of all if your skin is sensitive or easily irritated.

03
Step Three

Peptide or exosome serum: optional, and gentle

Peptides are small protein fragments that, in lab and early clinical studies, appear to signal skin to make more collagen. The evidence is thinner than it is for retinoids, but peptides are gentle, they work well with most other ingredients, and they are a nice addition on nights you are not using a retinoid or an exfoliant.

Apply a few drops after cleansing, or after your exfoliant, and give it a minute to sink in before the next step. My peptides guide explains how the different types work.

Exosome serums are a newer option in this step. Exosomes are tiny messengers that cells use to signal each other, and early studies suggest they may support repair and collagen, though the research is still at an early stage and products vary a lot. I use one regularly and wrote about what is and isn't known in my peptides, growth factors, and exosomes guide.

04
Step Four

Retinoid: the step worth being patient with

For fine lines, rough texture, and uneven tone, retinoids have the strongest evidence of any ingredient you can buy without a prescription. They speed up cell turnover and support collagen, which is why the results build over months rather than days. My retinol guide goes deeper on how they work.

Use your retinoid only at night, and only on nights you are not exfoliating. Start with two or three nights a week and a pea-sized amount for your whole face, applied to dry skin. If your skin stays comfortable for a couple of weeks, add another night. Some dryness and flaking early on is normal while your skin adjusts.

What the research shows

In a 2007 study in the Archives of Dermatology, people with an average age of 87 applied 0.4% retinol to one arm and a plain lotion to the other, up to three times a week for 24 weeks. The retinol side had noticeably fewer fine wrinkles, and skin samples showed signs of new collagen production.

The retinoid ladder

From gentlest to strongest, the main options are retinyl palmitate, retinol, retinaldehyde, and prescription tretinoin. Most women over 40 do well starting with a low-strength retinol or a retinaldehyde and moving up slowly. Tretinoin was too harsh for my skin, so I use a retinaldehyde, which I wrote about in my Crystal Retinal review.

05
Step Five

Moisturizer: richer at night

The last step seals in everything underneath and supports your barrier while you sleep. At night you can use a richer cream than you would under sunscreen, and ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids, and panthenol are all good signs on the label.

On retinoid and exfoliant nights, focus on calming and repairing rather than adding more actives. If your skin feels tight or stings, a soothing barrier cream on its own makes a perfectly good night.

The sandwich method

If retinoids irritate your skin, try a thin layer of moisturizer, then your retinoid, then more moisturizer on top. It slows how quickly the retinoid reaches your skin, which cuts down on redness and peeling while it keeps working.

A simple weekly schedule

This is a starting point for your first couple of months. Once your skin is comfortable, you can move up to five or six retinoid nights a week, keeping your exfoliant nights separate.

NightWhat to do
MondayCleanse, retinoid, moisturizer
TuesdayCleanse, peptide or exosome serum, moisturizer
WednesdayCleanse, exfoliant, moisturizer
ThursdayCleanse, retinoid, moisturizer
FridayCleanse, peptide or exosome serum, moisturizer
SaturdayCleanse, retinoid, moisturizer
SundayCleanse, peptide or exosome serum, a richer cream or overnight mask

Five minutes a night, with the strongest steps spread across the week, will do more for your skin than any single product.

The bottom line

Night is where the slower, repair-focused work of skincare happens. Clean your skin well, rotate your exfoliant and retinoid so they never land on the same night, and finish with a moisturizer that keeps your barrier comfortable. Give it a few months before judging the results.

If you are in perimenopause, the same plan applies with a little extra attention to barrier repair and a slower retinoid start. My morning routine covers the daytime half.
